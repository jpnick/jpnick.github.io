Policy Paper (2025)

Geospatial Analysis of Illicit Economies in Post-2021 Myanmar Coup d'état Using Nighttime Lights

The 2021 Myanmar coup d’état triggered widespread political and economic disruption. While the country as a whole experienced a decline in economic activity, certain regions saw a surge in illicit activities.

Given the limited availability of reliable data in conflict-affected settings, this study employs a Difference-in-Differences (DiD) approach using Nighttime Lights (NTL) data to assess post-coup changes in economic activity, particularly in areas suspected to be linked with transnational organized crimes (TOC).

National-level analysis indicates an overall decline in NTL following the coup. Moreover, specific locations associated with casinos and online scam centers—such as Shwe Kokko and Me Htaw Tha Lay in Kayin region, along with several areas in Shan region—exhibited statistically significant increases in NTL after the coup compared to other control areas within their respective regions.

Keywords: Myanmar, Coup d'état, Transnational organized crime, Scam centers, Cybercrime, Night-time light, Difference-in-differences, Remote Sensing

Motivation: Conflict & The Rise of Scams

Post-Coup Turmoil

Following the February 2021 coup, Myanmar plunged into a severe crisis. The conflict resulted in significant civilian casualties and mass displacement.

The economy contracted severely (est. 18% drop in GDP), disrupting trade and traditional economic activities countrywide.

The Illicit Boom

Paradoxically, as the formal economy collapsed, informal and illicit economies thrived in border regions, often under the control of militias or Transnational Organized Crime (TOC) syndicates.

  • Human Trafficking & Forced Labor
  • Industrial-scale Cyber Scams (e.g., "pig butchering")
  • Illegal Casinos & Money Laundering

Core Research Questions

1

Is there a measurable impact of the 2021 coup on overall economic activities?

2

Did informal and illicit economies in border regions significantly expand after the coup?

The Data Challenge: Tracking this is incredibly difficult due to the conflict, safety risks, and opaque governance. Ground data is unreliable or non-existent. Our solution? Look from space.

Methodology: NTL & DiD

Using Nighttime Lights (NTL) as a proxy for economic activity to bypass ground-level data scarcity.

Nighttime Lights (NTL)

Data from NASA (VIIRS/NPP). Serves as a reliable proxy for electricity use and economic activity.

Difference-in-Differences

Statistical method comparing the change in NTL in "Treated" (illicit hub) areas vs. "Control" areas, before and after 2021.

High-Resolution Mapping

Analyzing specific townships/wards (ADM3/ADM4) in conflict zones like Kayin (Myawaddy) and Shan states.

Empirical Results

1. National Economic Contraction

-0.217 DN

At the national level, Myanmar exhibited an average decline in Nighttime Lights (-0.217 Digital Number) following the 2021 coup. This aligns with broader economic indicators showing severe contraction, power outages, and disruption of normal economic activities across most of the country.

2. Explosive Growth in High-Risk Zones

Contrary to the national trend, specific treatment zones in both Kayin (e.g., Shwe Kokko, Me Htaw Tha Lay) and Shan regions experienced significantly greater increases in NTL compared to surrounding control areas.

Dynamic DiD (Event Study) Concept: Shwe Kokko / Kayin

Note: Parallel trends hold prior to 2021. After the coup, NTL intensity diverges sharply upwards in treated scam compound locations, indicating vast energy consumption from diesel generators to power massive server farms.

Conclusions & Policy Implications

The empirical evidence confirms that while Myanmar's formal economy suffered post-coup, isolated enclaves largely run by armed groups and TOCs underwent massive, highly localized expansion. This is visible from space via Nighttime Lights, highlighting the sheer scale of energy required to run these cyber-scam cities.

1

Leveraging Satellite Data

NTL data presents a critical opportunity for policymakers to bypass ground-level obfuscation. A spatial lens enables more precise, timely monitoring of where illicit economic hubs are growing.

2

Regional Cooperation

These compounds rely on cross-border logistics (e.g., smuggled diesel fuel, internet cables). Governments in Southeast Asia must enhance cooperation to cut off supply chains feeding these isolated light anomalies.
